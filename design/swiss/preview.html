00 — Orientation

Grid

The Swiss option. International Typographic Style applied straight: one typeface, a strict 12-column grid, dramatic scale contrast, and structure carried entirely by alignment and whitespace rather than by boxes.

Where Flight Deck says "this person measures things," Grid says "this person has judgement." It is quieter, more confident, and it ages better — the reference work is seventy years old and has not dated. It is also less immediately identifiable as an aerospace portfolio.

Three rules carry it: one typeface, no boxes, and alignment is meaning. Nothing is centred, there are no cards or panels or rounded corners, and the only drawn element in the whole system is a horizontal rule.

Every word and number below comes from the existing content files. Only project years are derived, read off the presentation venues. This option needs less derived content than Flight Deck — there is no readout bar, so the disputed "3+ yrs research" figure never has to appear at all.
01

Foundations

The grid

12 columns, 32px gutter, 1280px max. The standard split is content in columns 1–7 and meta in 9–12, with column 8 left empty. That gap is doing real work — it is what makes the layout read as deliberate rather than merely wide. Below 900px everything collapses to one column.

Column 8 — intentionally empty

Colour

Near-monochrome. When structure comes from alignment there is very little for colour to do: ink, paper, one red. The red appears two or three times per page — not per section.

--paperpage
--surfaceonly fill
--inktext and rules
--ink-muted8.4:1
--ink-subtle4.9:1 · floor
--accent2–3 per page

Type — one family

Inter at 400/500/600/700/800. No monospace anywhere and no second family for headings; all differentiation comes from size, weight and position. Space Grotesk and JetBrains Mono get dropped from the build, so adopting this is a net payload saving.

The 7.5× jump from display to body is the drama. Do not compress it.

Space

128px between sections at mobile, 192px at desktop — roughly double Flight Deck. Whitespace is the primary structural device here, so under-spacing breaks this system in a way it does not break the other one. If the gaps feel excessive they are probably correct.

02

Components

Six, against Flight Deck's nine. The reduction is the point — most of what that system solves with a component, this one solves with the grid.

WorkEntry

Year in columns 1–2, title in 3–9, tags stacked in 10–12. Rule beneath. Hover turns the title red and nothing moves. Tags are stacked text — there is no chip in this system.
